Know Exactly Where Your Fills Are Costing You.

Day traders live and die by execution. SignalDeck measures it on every single trade.

Entry Slippage

The difference between your intended entry and your actual fill. Identifies whether your market orders are costing you edge at open.

Exit Slippage

How much you're giving back on exit. High exit slippage on winning trades means your actual R is lower than planned.

MAE (Adverse Excursion)

How far a trade moved against you before turning profitable. Reveals whether your stops are too tight or your entries too early.

MFE (Favorable Excursion)

The maximum profit available on each trade before you exited. If MFE consistently exceeds your actual exit, you're leaving R on the table.

More Questions

What is execution quality analysis for day traders?

Execution quality analysis measures the difference between your intended entry/exit and your actual fill. Entry slippage tells you how much you're paying above the intended price on market orders. Exit slippage shows how much you're giving back at close. Limit alpha measures the edge you capture by using limit orders. MAE (Maximum Adverse Excursion) and MFE (Maximum Favorable Excursion) show you how far trades moved against and in your favor before closing — identifying whether you're exiting too early or holding losers too long.

How does behavioral tagging help day traders?

Behavioral tags let you mark the psychological driver behind each trade — FOMO, hesitation, revenge trading, overconfidence, or following the plan. Over time, the journal shows you which tags correlate with negative R-multiples, which times of day produce your worst behavioral trades, and whether specific market conditions trigger predictable mistakes. This turns psychology from a vague problem into a measurable, trackable pattern.
